# Independent audit: v1.5 external first pass (MIMIC-IV, UK Biobank)

Auditor: read-only. The only file written is this report, plus small check scripts in the same scratchpad
directory. All numbers below are aggregates. No patient rows or identifiers were printed.

## Summary of severity

| # | Severity | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Major** (borderline critical for inference) | The pooled "20/26, p = 0.009" treats 26 emulations as independent, but they cover only 18 RCTs. Clustered by RCT: 13/18, sign p = 0.096; mean Δ\|Δlog HR\| sign-flip p = 0.17; z² sign-flip p = 0.029. |
| 2 | **Major** | MIMIC: index-admission discharge diagnoses are used as baseline (sparse dx and the hdPS panel). hdPS ranks features on exposure alone, and it selects post-t0 or exposure-caused codes into hdPS200. |
| 3 | **Major** | MIMIC PLATO: the MI component counts I21 codes carried forward from the index MI. About 60–75% of first "MI readmissions" fall within 28 days, and most are in a secondary diagnosis position. |
| 4 | **Major** | UKB: the ECG is recorded at the same visit that defines prevalent exposure, so it is an on-treatment covariate. This matters most in ASCOT, where beta-blockers change the ECG directly. |
| 5 | **Major** | UKB: MI outcomes come from first-occurrence dates (41280/42000). New MI is largely unobservable in people with a prior MI, and prior MI is very unequal between arms in ASCOT (1% vs 18% with I21). |
| 6 | **Major** (claim accuracy) | The claim that the engine "reproduces the Yale estimates exactly" on LIFE is not supported by the artefacts on disk. Sparse differs by −0.065 log HR (0.55 SE), even with Yale's own imputed baseline. |
| 7–15 | Minor | See below. |

No critical data-handling bug was found in the main mechanics: time zero, ECG timing, arm orientation, benchmarks, pid linkage, lead order/units, imputation, matching/Cox, and the summary numbers are all correct.

## Major findings

### M1. Pooled 26-emulation claim overstates evidence (non-independence)
- **Where:** `docs/V15_EXTERNAL_SUMMARY.md:43-59`.
- **Recomputation.** Yale rows come from `claude-v13-bootstrap/bs_<trial>.csv` rep 0, 18 trials: the 10 PRIMARY trials plus emperor-preserved-v2, east-afnet4, cabana-v2, ontarget, value, ascot, empa-reg and carolina, with benchmarks from `v13_common.bench`. External rows come from `estimates.csv`, population full, outcome primary, with `rct.json` benchmarks (identical to `bench(key)`). Script: `scratchpad/pool.py`.
- **The coordinator's numbers reproduce:**
  - C1: 20/26, sign p = 0.009, mean Δ\|Δ\| = −0.031 (MC sign-flip p 0.055 vs 0.058 stated), mean Δz² = −1.11 (p 0.007 vs 0.009).
  - Yale 18 and External 8 match exactly.
  - C2: 15/26, 0.56, −0.011 (0.46), −0.46 (0.17) match.
  - The small p differences are Monte Carlo noise, because `v13_summarize.sign_flip` uses 20,000 random flips when n > 16. The "exact sign-flip" label at `:46` is therefore wrong for the 26- and 18-trial rows (minor).
- **Independence problem.** Eight RCTs appear twice: PLATO, ARISTOTLE, ROCKET AF, TRANSFORM-HF and COMET (Yale + MIMIC), and ONTARGET, ALLHAT and ASCOT (Yale + UKB). They share the benchmark error (log RCT HR). The Yale-18 set already contains all 8, so the external cohorts add replication of the same benchmark draws, not new RCTs.
- **Fairer alternatives for C1 (sparse+ECG vs sparse):**

  | Approach | n | Closer | Sign p | Mean Δ\|Δ\| (sf p) | Mean Δz² (sf p) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | As claimed (26 independent) | 26 | 20 | 0.009 | −0.031 (0.055) | −1.11 (0.007) |
  | **RCT-clustered: mean of Δ within RCT, flip at RCT level** | 18 | 13 | **0.096** | −0.027 (**0.17**) | −1.34 (**0.029**) |
  | One per RCT, Yale version | 18 | 14 | 0.031 | −0.020 (0.27) | −1.40 (0.024) |
  | One per RCT, external version where available | 18 | 13 | 0.096 | −0.034 (0.16) | −1.28 (0.049) |
  | Overlap RCTs only (both cohorts) | 16 | 13 | 0.021 | −0.041 (0.031) | −0.59 (0.013) |

  C2 clustered by RCT: 9/18, p = 1.0; Δ\|Δ\| +0.002 (0.89); Δz² −0.27 (0.43).
- **Suggested fix:**
  - Make the RCT-clustered sign-flip the headline: average the paired Δ within each RCT, then do an exact sign-flip over 18 clusters (2^18 is enumerable, so it can be exact).
  - Report the 26-emulation count as descriptive only.
  - Also report a paired "replication" statistic: for the 8 overlapping RCTs, does the Yale and external Δ have the same sign? C1: 5/8 same sign. The discordant ones are TRANSFORM-HF (Yale +0.006, MIMIC −0.124), COMET (Yale −0.004, MIMIC +0.038) and ASCOT (Yale −0.031, UKB +0.113).
  - Mark the whole pooled analysis post hoc.
  - With clustering, the conclusion softens. The count and |Δ| evidence is no longer significant; only the z² metric stays below 0.05, and it is heavily driven by Yale cabana-v2 (Δz² −10.1), paradigm-hf-seq (−4.8) and value (−4.6).

### M2. MIMIC: index-admission discharge diagnoses used as baseline; post-t0 and exposure-caused codes enter hdPS200
- **Where:**
  - `scripts/v15/v15_mimic_cohort.py:100-104,166-168`: the sparse dx concepts use `cdx`, which includes `is_index` admissions.
  - `:265-269`: the panel dx includes the index admission's full discharge code list.
  - The note at `:356` acknowledges that these "may include post-t0 events".
  - The hdPS ranking (`eval_longtail_balance.hdps_rank:98-101`) orders levels by |log prevalence ratio| between arms, without using the outcome. This ranking preferentially pulls in codes caused by the treatment.
- **Evidence** (aggregate prevalence apixaban/warfarin etc. in the analysed ECG cohort, hdPS rank). Script: `scratchpad/chk_hd2.py`.
  - **ARISTOTLE:**
    - `dx_i9_E93` (E934.x, adverse effect of anticoagulants in therapeutic use): 1.0% vs 7.2%, rank 30.
    - `dx_i9_790` (790.92, abnormal coagulation profile / high INR): 1.7% vs 8.0%, rank 72.
    - `dx_i9_V58` (includes V58.61, long-term anticoagulant use): 8.5% vs 32.8%, rank 116.
    - `dx_I63`: 11.4% vs 3.1%, rank 135.
    - All four are inside hdPS200.
  - **ROCKET AF:** `dx_i9_790` rank 83 (2.5% vs 9.8%), inside hdPS200.
  - **PLATO:**
    - `dx_I46` (cardiac arrest): 3.9% vs 0.9%, rank 58.
    - `dx_Y84`: rank 120.
    - Both are in hdPS200.
    - `dx_Z79` (includes Z79.02, long-term antiplatelet): 31% vs 20%, rank 426, outside hdPS200.
  - **TRANSFORM-HF:** `dx_R57` (shock) rank 93, `dx_I46` rank 106, `dx_Z51` (palliative care encounter) rank 154. All are in hdPS200, and all are strong death predictors that are often coded after t0.
  - **COMET:** `dx_R79` rank 41, `dx_I61` rank 34.
- **Impact:**
  - Some of these codes are pre-t0 confounders, for example cardiac surgery or a stroke that led to anticoagulation. Others are mediators or effects of the drug: INR abnormalities and anticoagulant adverse effects in warfarin users, and palliative decisions or shock after initiation.
  - hdPS arms (M3/M4, and therefore C2) are the most exposed.
  - Sparse and C1 are affected through `stroke_history`, `heart_failure`, `prior_bleed` and similar concepts that include index-admission codes.
  - In ICD-9-era data, the ICD-9/ICD-10 split also makes many codes calendar proxies.
  - Direction is unknowable without a sensitivity analysis. It could plausibly change hdPS-arm estimates in the AF trials, where there are few events (18 and 11 in the treated arms).
- **Suggested fix:** as a pre-specified sensitivity analysis, rebuild the dx covariates and dx panel from earlier admissions only (dropping `is_index`).
  - Also consider keeping only index-admission codes that are plausibly present on admission, for example the gate and chronic conditions.
  - Exclude, at minimum, E93x/Y4x/T45/T36-T50, D68.3x, R79/790.9x, V58.6x/Z79 and Z51/Z66 (plus R57/I46 when they come from the index admission) from the panel.
  - Report C1/C2 under both versions.

### M3. MIMIC PLATO: MI component inflated by carry-forward I21 codes
- **Where:**
  - `v15_mimic_common.py:56`: `out_mi` = I21/I22, ICD-9 410.
  - `v15_mimic_outcomes.py:52-56`: any diagnosis position of any later admission, dated to the admission date.
- **Evidence** (ECG-analysed PLATO cohort; script `scratchpad/plato_mi.py`):
  - First MI-coded readmission within 365 d: clopidogrel 315, of which 185 were within 28 d and 158 of those were secondary-position only. Ticagrelor 52, of which 39 were within 28 d and 31 secondary-position.
  - ICD-10 coding rules assign I21 for 4 weeks after an acute MI, so most of these are the index MI, not a new event.
  - The index MI share differs sharply by arm: dx_I21 in the index admission was 74% for ticagrelor vs 35% for clopidogrel.
  - So the misclassification is correlated with a strong arm-differential characteristic. For comparison, the ARISTOTLE stroke/SE outcome has fewer such early events (24/81 and 4/18 within 28 d).
- **Suggested fix:**
  - Count a readmission as MI only if the admission starts more than 28 days after index and has I21/I22 (ICD-9 410.x1) in position 1.
  - Within 28 days, count only I22 or primary-position codes.
  - Apply the same rule to stroke: I63 within 28 d of an index stroke admission. This matters in ARISTOTLE, where index I63 was 11% vs 3%.
  - Re-run PLATO, and ideally ARISTOTLE and ROCKET AF.

### M4. UKB: the ECG is measured on treatment (prevalent users, lag 0 at the exposure-defining visit)
- **Where:**
  - `v15_ukb_build.py:200-202`: ECG = 20205 instance 2, the same visit as the 20003 self-report that defines the arm.
  - `v15_ukb_ecg_embed.py:117-138`.
- **Impact:**
  - In a prevalent-user design, the ECG reflects the drug's own effect: beta-blockers lower heart rate and prolong PR; thiazides lower potassium and change QT. So "sparse+ECG" partly adjusts for a consequence of treatment, a mediator or instrument-like variable.
  - This is most acute in ASCOT (amlodipine vs beta-blocker). There the ECG-only PS AUC is 0.672, vs 0.569 in ONTARGET and 0.634 in ALLHAT, and C1 goes the wrong way (+0.113).
  - The protocol states the prevalent-user limitation, but not that the ECG itself is post-treatment. The same applies to SBP at instance 2.
  - This undermines interpreting the UKB C1/C2 contrasts as "ECG adds confounding information".
- **Suggested fix:**
  - Label the UKB ECG as an on-treatment covariate.
  - Run a sensitivity analysis that excludes ASCOT from pooled ECG claims, or residualises the ECG on known drug effects. This is hard; heart rate would be the minimum.
  - If feasible, use a new-user variant: exposure at instance 3 among instance-2 non-users, with the instance-2 ECG as the pre-treatment ECG. 10,962 instance-3 ECGs exist.

### M5. UKB: MI outcome ascertainment depends on prior MI (first-occurrence data)
- **Where:**
  - `v15_ukb_build.py:118-122,129-132`: `first_after` over 41270/41280 first-occurrence dates plus 42000 (first MI only).
  - The note at `:172` acknowledges this.
- **Evidence:** in the ASCOT analysed cohort, dx_I21 before index was 1% (amlodipine) vs 18% (beta-blocker), with I25 at 5% vs 40%.
  - A new MI in someone already coded I21.x is observable only through a different 4-character subcode or I22.
  - So MI outcomes are systematically under-ascertained in the beta-blocker arm.
  - Matching on the broad `dx_ihd_mi` (I20–I25 + algorithmic MI) does not ensure balance on prior I21 specifically in the sparse arms.
  - ALLHAT and ONTARGET are affected less (prior I21 around 5–7% and similar between arms).
- **Suggested fix:**
  - Use HESIN episode-level data if available.
  - Otherwise restrict MI-outcome trials to people without prior MI. That is ASCOT's own eligibility; `SPEC_ELIG` already has it and it leaves 3,157/1,118 with an ECG.
  - At minimum, add an exact prior-I21 covariate to sparse, and report ASCOT under the ASCOT eligibility.

### M6. Engine LIFE validation claim not supported by on-disk evidence
- **Where:** `docs/V15_EXTERNAL_SUMMARY.md:39-41` says the engine "reproduces the Yale estimates exactly when given the same design and patients".
- **Evidence:** `claude-v15-validate-life/analysis/compare_yale.csv` and `fixture-yale-imp1/analysis/compare_yale.csv`:

  | Arm | Result |
  |---|---|
  | unmatched | Identical (so the same 3,126 patients are used) |
  | sparse | −0.2202 vs Yale −0.1555: diff −0.065 (0.55 SE), 1237 vs 1239 pairs, in both fixtures (including Yale's completed imputation) |
  | sparse+ECG | +0.022 |
  | hdPS200 | −0.001 |
  | hdPS200+ECG | −0.005 |
  | clinical | −0.022, or +0.059 with Yale imp1 |

- **Why the explanation does not fit:**
  - The sparse arm contains no imputed values or hdPS features.
  - "5 excluded patients" is contradicted by the identical unmatched estimate.
  - So the stated explanation (single imputation, all-feature hdPS, 5 excluded patients) does not account for the sparse gap.
  - A likely cause is the fixture's dx list: `v15_validate_yale_life.py:47` drops `pci_index_30d`, and Yale's `X_dx` = demo + all `dxc`. That is a design difference, not "the same design".
- **Suggested fix:**
  - Build a fixture with Yale's exact `X_dx` columns and Yale's hdPS candidate set, and show bit-identical loghr for sparse, sparse+ECG and hdPS200.
  - Until then, reword the claim as: "reproduces unmatched exactly; matched arms within 0.07 log HR (≤ 0.55 SE)".

## Minor findings

1. **"Exact" sign-flip is Monte Carlo for n > 16** (`v13_summarize.py:229-236`; summary `:46`). Use full enumeration: 2^26 ≈ 6.7e7 is feasible, 2^18 is trivial. Alternatively, label the result MC with its seed.
2. **MIMIC censoring rule is effectively inert** (`v15_mimic_outcomes.py:46-50,68`).
   - `cens_day` = max(discharge over all admissions, including the index) + 365. That is always ≥ 365 ≥ H, so no one is censored before the horizon; the share of e=0 rows with t = H is 1.0 for PLATO, TRANSFORM-HF and COMET.
   - This is actually correct: dod is captured to 1 year after the last discharge, the index discharge is ≥ t0, and ascertainment is symmetric between arms. But the protocol wording implies censoring that never happens.
   - The rule also uses post-index admissions. That is harmless here but not good practice. Document it as "follow-up complete to 365 d by construction".
3. **MIMIC analysed populations are effectively ≤ 2019** because MIMIC-IV-ECG coverage ends then. ECG coverage by estimated index year is about 85–90% up to 2017, 42–62% in 2018–20, and 0% from 2021.
   - This is why apixaban ECG coverage is 30% vs 69% for warfarin, and ticagrelor 48% vs 65%.
   - Within year bands, coverage is similar by arm, so this is not selection by arm. But `index_year` is noisy (anchor-group midpoint ±1.5 y), so calendar adjustment is imprecise.
   - It also discards about half of each cohort. The summary should state that the analysed N is the ECG subset.
4. **MIMIC "new user" means new to BIDMC inpatient orders.** Home warfarin or clopidogrel continued on admission counts as initiation (acknowledged at `v15_mimic_cohort.py:353`). V58.6x/Z79 prevalence suggests many warfarin-arm patients are long-term users. Consider a sensitivity analysis that excludes patients with long-term anticoagulant/antiplatelet codes in earlier admissions, or with an eMAR "home med" flag.
5. **TRANSFORM-HF washout hole** (`v15_mimic_cohort.py:81`). For TRANSFORM-HF the same-admission exclusion is only `e.starttime < t0 AND e.is_oral`, so index-admission orders with a NULL starttime (any route) are not excluded, unlike the other trials. The effect is probably tiny; add `OR e.starttime IS NULL AND e.is_oral`.
6. **COMET arm** includes metoprolol succinate/Toprol in MIMIC; the trial used tartrate. This is disclosed. Consider a tartrate-only sensitivity analysis.
7. **MIMIC PLATO has 36 stale ECG rows.** The PLATO `ecg_embedding.parquet` holds 2,910 rows, but only 2,874 are in the rebuilt cohort. The ECG prepare step ran before the cohort rebuild; the pipeline skips prepare when `prepare_summary.json` exists (`v15_mimic_ecg_pipeline.py:312`).
   - The engine inner-joins (`v15_analyze.py:112`), so the extra rows are dropped.
   - For the retained pids, the selected ECG equals the latest in-window ECG under the current `index_time` for 2,866/2,874; the rest are the documented fallbacks. No selected ECG postdates t0 in any trial.
   - Harmless, but `ecg_summary.json` for PLATO reports "linked 2910". Regenerate or annotate it.
8. **UKB plasmode censoring** (`v15_analyze.py:122-123`). `C_i = horizon` for patients with events ignores their administrative end (2022-10-31 minus the visit date, median about 3.7 y vs horizons of 4.7–5.5 y). This inflates simulated events for late visitors. Use min(horizon, admin end) as C_i.
9. **Plasmode outcome model excludes the ECG** (`v15_analyze.py:352-378`; inherited from Yale). ECG arms can reduce bias only through correlation with the clinical covariates, so C1 plasmode reductions near zero are expected by construction. State this in the summary.
10. **`pc1_sd` differs by definition.** `v15_ukb_ecg_embed.py:81` computes `pc1_sd` as `sv[0]/sqrt(len(sv))` (256), while the MIMIC `emb_stats` divides by sqrt(n). The values (41.5 vs 4.6) are not comparable. Cosmetic.
11. **CLMBR coverage wording.** `V15_EXTERNAL_SUMMARY.md:35` says "about 67–76%"; the observed subset shares are 73.5%, 73.0% and 75.8%. Minor wording.
12. **ELITE II secondary benchmark is uninformative.** Matched events are about 11 vs <11. It should be marked as such.
13. **UKB hypertension gate is non-binding.** Every arm drug is an antihypertensive, so the gate drops no one (8,758 → 8,758 in ONTARGET). This is fine, but the gate should not be described as a restriction.

## Checked and found fine

- **Time zero and ECG timing (MIMIC).** For all 5 trials, recomputed from `record_list.csv` against the current `index_time`:
  - 0 selected ECGs after t0; `lag_days` range 0–365.
  - Selected ECG = latest in-window ECG except the documented fallbacks (8–16 per trial).
  - `index_day` in `cohort.parquet` equals `index_time` exactly; `treated` agrees; outcomes pids = cohort pids.
- **MIMIC WFDB conversion** (`v15_mimic_ecg_convert.py`):
  - Leads are reordered by header name; units come from the header; resampling is applied only when fs ≠ 500.
  - In the validation JSON, Einthoven/Goldberger residuals and per-lead amplitudes match Yale (for example, aVL residual 0.113 vs 0.121).
  - 18,034 npy files are on disk, matching the summary.
- **UKB ECG** (independent check on 200 random instance-2 files):
  - Shape (12, 5000) float32.
  - Identity residuals: III 0.17, aVR 0.05, aVL 0.14, aVF 0.10. Swapping aVL/aVF gives 1.4–1.7, so the order is I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1–V6.
  - Amplitudes are in mV and Yale-like.
  - 93,262 files, all embedded (instances 2 + 3).
  - Linkage pid = eid, instance 2, deduplicated.
- **Embedding validation:** MIMIC and UKB mean norm, mean unit norm, PC spectra and PCs-for-90% are close to Yale.
- **Arm orientation and benchmarks:**
  - All 8 `rct.json` files match `trial_specs.PUBLISHED`.
  - COMET is inverted correctly: our_orientation 1.205 (1.075–1.351), metoprolol = treated; MIMIC HRs are consistent (unmatched 1.41).
  - The ELITE II 95.7% CI is converted properly to 95% (0.95–1.34).
  - `v15_summarize.bench` equals `v13_common.bench`.
- **Washout logic.** There are no earlier-admission orders of either drug, and no index-admission orders before t0 (except TRANSFORM IV, which is allowed, and the minor hole in item 5). Ties are excluded. The calendar floor is applied.
- **Exposure tokens:**
  - MIMIC rx panels contain no arm tokens, including brands; there is an assert at `v15_mimic_cohort.py:291`.
  - UKB rx panels contain no ARB/ACEi (ONTARGET), amlodipine/thiazide/other DHP (ALLHAT) or beta-blocker/amlodipine (ASCOT) tokens.
  - UKB meds roles drop the exposure classes.
  - The remaining exposure leakage is via dx codes (M2).
- **UKB timing and censoring:**
  - Covariates use dates strictly before index. Biomarkers come from instance 0/1.
  - Events are strictly after index. Censoring = min(death, 2022-10-31, horizon), and death after the data end is ignored symmetrically.
  - Horizons are not capped (maximum possible follow-up 3,106 d).
- **CLMBR:**
  - censor_date = imaging date for 41,842/41,844.
  - The leakage probe gives an AUC of 0.53–0.77 among arm-naive participants, and the CLMBR-only PS AUC (0.79) is below the sparse PS AUC (0.83) in ASCOT. There is no sign that the exposure is encoded.
  - The subset view re-runs the base arms like-for-like.
- **Engine** (`v15_analyze.py`):
  - The imputer uses covariates only, with no treatment or outcome, bounded to the observed range.
  - Missing-indicators are in the design but not in the outcome model.
  - Design matrices per arm match the protocol (sparse = demo+dx; hdPS = sparse + top-200 re-ranked per subsample; ECG = 32 PCs; clinical = all groups).
  - Matching and Cox use the audited `v13_common` functions. The unmatched arm is unclustered.
  - Balance uses observed values only.
  - Plasmode truth comes from counterfactual copies with common random numbers in each arm's matched population. The 80% subsample is without replacement. The MC CIs for bias reduction are paired across arms (same bootstrap indices).
  - The RCT-DUPLICATE metrics use standard definitions: estimate agreement = within the RCT CI; φ = Q/(k−1); DerSimonian–Laird ratio of ratios.
- **Outcome code lists.** MIMIC stroke/SE, MI and NCOs, and UKB MI/stroke/HF, match `trial_specs`. `nco_v2` left the primary columns unchanged (asserted by `assert_frame_equal`, and `outcomes_preNCO` is kept).
- **Spot-check of `V15_EXTERNAL_SUMMARY.md` against the CSVs.** 64 HR-table cells (HR, CI, pairs, N) were checked with 0 mismatches. Also checked and all matching:

  - the C1/C2/ECGonly per-trial Δ values (via the pooled recomputation);
  - 18,034 and 93,262 file counts.
