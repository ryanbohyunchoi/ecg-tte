1. Bottom line

Question: when a propensity score (PS) is built from structured EHR data, does adding the patient's raw 12-lead ECG (as an embedding) improve confounding control — and does that make emulated trial results agree better with the randomized trials?

In one sentence. The ECG supplies cardiac-structure information that the coded record does not (it clearly improves balance on measured physiology), but the gain in effect estimates is small — a real but tiny bias reduction for a sparse PS in simulation, a consistent-direction but non-significant move toward the RCT results — and far below what benchmarking against RCTs can detect.

2. How to read this

Key terms, in plain language. Hover over most chart elements for exact numbers; column headers sort tables.

Target trial emulation (TTE)
Re-creating a randomized trial's question in EHR data: same kind of patients, same two treatments started at a clear "time zero", same outcome.
Propensity score (PS) and matching
A model of the probability of receiving treatment A (vs B) given baseline characteristics. Patients are paired 1:1 on this score so the two groups look alike on what went into the model.
Sparse PS (M1)
Demographics + a list of selected diagnoses only. Stands in for a minimal, "data-poor" adjustment set.
hdPS200 (M3)
High-dimensional PS: sparse + the 200 coded features (diagnoses, drugs, procedures, lab-test flags) most imbalanced between arms, chosen automatically.
+ ECG (M2, M4)
32 principal components of the pre-trained BCL ECG encoder's embedding of the patient's most recent pre-index ECG (the "unstructured" information).
Clinical PS (R)
Investigator-chosen covariates including labs, vitals and EF — the best conventional adjustment set; used as a reference.
R+ (physiology reference)
R plus every echo measure, NT-proBNP and labs. Estimates from the same data, so comparing to R+ removes RCT-vs-emulation design differences.
Balance / capture %
After matching, how different are the arms on characteristics not in the PS (e.g. echo LV structure)? Measured as excess |standardized difference| above chance. Capture % = share of the unmatched imbalance removed.
Hazard ratio (HR), log HR
Relative rate of the outcome, arm A vs arm B. We compare on the log scale: |log HR difference| of 0.10 ≈ a 10% relative difference in HR.
Agreement with the RCT
Estimate agreement = our HR inside the RCT's CI; std-difference agreement = |(our − RCT)/combined SE| < 1.96; mean |Δlog HR| = average distance.
C1, C2
The two contrasts of interest: C1 = sparse+ECG vs sparse; C2 = hdPS200+ECG vs hdPS200.
Plasmode simulation
Keep the real patients and their real treatment choices, but simulate outcomes from a known model with a known true effect — so bias can be measured exactly.
Paired bootstrap
Re-sample patients 200 times, redo PS + matching + Cox each time, to get the uncertainty of the difference between two arms.
Multiverse
Re-run everything under 200 reasonable analysis choices (PS model, caliper, 1:1/1:3, weighting, hdPS split) to check a result is not an artifact of one choice.

3. Study design & timeline

Design

  • New-user, active-comparator emulations on the Yale OMOP EHR (2013–2024), all initiators.
  • Arms M0–M4 + R (+ R+ from v1.3); 1:1 greedy caliper matching (0.2 SD of logit PS).
  • Phase 1 (balance) was run and frozen before any outcome was looked at (protocol v1, v1.1).
  • Phase 2 (hazard ratios, Cox, robust SE, 5 imputations) run once (v1.2).
  • v1.3 and v1.4: exploratory robustness and mechanism analyses, each registered before running, with dated deviation logs.
Protocol v1 (frozen) — trials, arms, balance metrics, hypotheses H1–H5.
v1.1 — audit fixes: exact matcher, sequential PARADIGM-HF design, echo evaluation from 2016-07-31.
v1.2 — outcome-code fixes; phase 2 run once → H5 (better RCT agreement) not supported.
v1.3 — robustness: plasmode, bootstrap, multiverse, design fidelity, NCO calibration, 8 new trials (18 total).
v1.4 — where does the ECG add? data-poor patients, code dropout, no-echo, echo-physiology plasmode, HF-hospitalisation outcome.

5. Phase 1 — balance on characteristics the PS did not use

Capture % = share of the pre-matching imbalance removed by matching (100% = fully balanced; 0% = no better than unmatched; negative = worse). Blank = nothing to capture in that trial. This is where the ECG's signal is clearest.

Values above 100% mean the post-matching imbalance fell below what chance alone would produce; they are shown as 100+. Values below −100% are shown as ≤−100. Hover for exact values.
Read across the bar chart: for the echo domains (LV structure, LV function, diastolic, RV), adding the ECG moves sparse and hdPS200 most of the way to — or past — the clinical PS. Valves are the exception: the ECG does not see valve disease.

6. Phase 2 — effect estimates

Forest plot per trial

Agreement with the RCT, all trials

Why a lower mean |Δ| is not "demonstrated" agreement

Each dot is one trial: how much closer (negative) or farther (positive) the ECG arm is from the RCT than its comparator, in |log HR| units. The mean of the dots is the headline improvement. The bar shows its 95% CI (t-interval across trials). If the bar crosses zero, the improvement is not distinguishable from chance — even though the mean is on the "better" side.

7. Robustness program (protocol v1.3)

Plasmode: bias with a known true effect

Real cohorts, real treatment assignment, simulated outcomes (true conditional HR 0.8, or 1.0 in "null"). Each replicate takes an 80% subsample of patients without replacement and refits PS + matching (valid for matching estimators; primary after the audit). The registered fixed-matched-set version and the bootstrap-resampled version are shown for comparison. Bars: reduction in |bias| when the ECG is added (positive = ECG arm less biased), with 95% Monte Carlo CI.

Scenario definitions
  • base: outcome depends on all clinical covariates as fitted to the real outcome.
  • phys_only: only demographics, diagnoses and core-9 physiology drive the outcome (medications and utilisation switched off).
  • strong: physiology coefficients doubled. none: physiology switched off. null: true HR 1.0.

Paired bootstrap on real data

Audit note. These intervals come from a within-trial bootstrap that re-matches on resamples containing duplicate patients, which is not valid for matching estimators. They are shown for description only; the valid tests are the exact across-trial sign-flip tests in section 8b (hover a row for its sign-flip p).

Statistic = mean over trials of [error(ECG arm)² − error(comparator)²] against the chosen target. Negative = ECG arm closer.

Multiverse (200 analysis specifications)

Distribution over specifications of [mean error of ECG arm − mean error of comparator] across trials. Left of zero = ECG arm better in that specification. Specifications are highly correlated (same data), so the share is a robustness check, not a significance test.

Dose response, supervised SHD scores, second encoder

Closer to the trial design

Mean |Δlog HR| vs the RCT per arm under each design refinement (lower = closer). None improved agreement overall; transport, strict outcomes and the run-in lose events and effective sample size.

Negative-control outcomes and empirical calibration

25 outcomes with no plausible drug effect (e.g. cataract surgery, ingrown nail). Their HRs estimate each arm's systematic error: μ = average bias, σ = spread. Adding the ECG did not reduce either.

8. Where does the ECG add information? (protocol v1.4)

Pre-registered tests of the settings where the ECG should matter most: thin coded records, codes deliberately deleted, no echo available, echo physiology as the hidden confounder, and a physiology-driven outcome.

8b. RCT-DUPLICATE metric panel, blinded closeness, and valid tests (after the audit)

The metrics RCT-DUPLICATE (Wang, JAMA 2023) reports, per PS specification, plus the ratio of ratios and Heyard's dispersion φ (excess disagreement over what sampling error predicts; 1 = none). Closeness was rated with the published rubric by a rater who saw only the trial protocols and our emulation specifications, not our results.

Paired comparisons with valid inference (exact sign-flip across trials)

Blinded closeness rating (RCT-DUPLICATE rubric)

Sensitivity analysis: CLMBR EHR-foundation-model embeddings (with and without ECG)

CLMBR-T code-only patient embeddings (64 PCs) — a representation of the full coded record, not new information. Full-cohort estimates, 18 trials; Pearson r with Fisher 95% CI as in RCT-DUPLICATE.

How to read this. RCT-DUPLICATE reported agreement descriptively across several metrics and found it depended mostly on how closely the design could be emulated (r 0.93 in close vs 0.53 in other emulations). Our panel shows the same: the clinical PS agrees very well in the 5 blinded-close trials. The ECG's measurable contribution is a reduction in excess disagreement for PSs lacking clinical detail — sparse φ 4.7 → 3.4 (sign-flip p = 0.024 on precision-standardised disagreement), ECG alone vs no adjustment p = 0.002 — concentrated in trials that are not closely emulated.

10. Limitations & next steps

  • RCT benchmarks mix design error with confounding. Population, adherence, outcome definitions and dose differ; this swamps small confounding improvements (≈0.12–0.15 log-HR differences even for the best PS).
  • Orders, not dispensings. No days-supply; per-protocol analyses are approximate; dose not identifiable.
  • Imputed physiology. Core-9 values are imputed when missing; the plasmode physiology partly reflects imputation, which is why v1.4 D uses measured echo values.
  • One health system, data-rich patients. hdPS works best here; the ECG's niche may be data-poor settings (v1.4 A–C).
  • Exploratory analyses after seeing phase 2. Registered before running, with deviation logs, but no multiplicity correction.
